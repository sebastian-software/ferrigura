# 0023 A design mode beside the reference's look

**Scope:** the per-render option that draws a diagram in ferrigura's own look and with its own measures, what both change, and how the mode relates to the compatibility switch. It does not change parsing, Mermaid configuration, text measurement or the layout engines.

## Decision

`RenderOptions.design` selects the design mode. It defaults to `false`, and an absent option preserves the output byte for byte. Node.js takes `design: true`; the CLI takes `--design`. It is a render option, not a Mermaid configuration key: front matter and init directives cannot set it.

The mode has its own look and its own measures. The look is paint: colors, outlines, corners, arrowheads. The measures change what the layout engine is given: node sizes, spacings, paddings and options. The engines themselves stay exact ports ([0012](./0012-layout-engines-ported-exactly.md)), and the mode starts from the optimized default's layout input ([0019](./0019-adaptive-flowchart-mode.md)).

- **Colors are theme variables.** The mode lays its own values for the shared color tokens under the user's `themeVariables`, which win key by key. Elements and custom properties keep reading the theme tokens ([0011](./0011-themes-as-css-variables.md)). It has a light and a dark set: the color scheme chooses, and without one the configured theme's name does.
- **Color follows a node's role, not its order.** A shape with a conventional meaning carries a `data-role` attribute that the style sheet colors. In a flowchart: `terminal` for the stadium and the circles, `decision` for the diamond, `data` for the cylinder. In a state diagram: `terminal` for the start and the end state, `decision` for the choice, and `junction` for the fork and the join bar, which take the color of the lines they join. The roles are the diagram type's: a shape both types can draw carries the role of the type it is drawn in. Other shapes stay neutral. The theme's palette is not used: groups are neutral.
- **Outlines are hairlines.** A node outline is 1.25 px and an edge 1.5 px, so that nodes stand in front of their connections. A thick edge is 3 px.
- **Edges are drawn anew through the points of their route.** A rounded edge turns on a radius of 10 instead of 5, also where it hops over another edge. It is drawn without the points the layout leaves on its straight parts: a corner takes at most half of each part that meets in it, so a point shortly before a corner would make that turn tighter than the others. The arrowhead is slimmer than the reference's and notched at the back; its tip stays on the end of the path. An edge label interrupts its edge: the edges are masked in the label's box, which is its text's box and 5 more on each side with a corner radius of 6, so the label needs no background and reads on any ground. The layout is given that box's width and 6 of room at both ends, so that it does not touch a node beside it. A group's frame is pulled in around its members and the boxes of the labels of its inner edges after the layout; where it still crosses a label's box, the label slides along the straight part of its edge, the shorter way, until the box is 6 clear of the frame.
- **A node gives its edges room.** A node grows along the side its edges meet until its busiest side has 20 for each edge and 24 at both ends: along its height in a layout from left to right, along its width from top to bottom. The edges into a node count for one side, the edges out of it for the other. A shape that takes no size of its own, such as the stadium and the diamond, stays as it is.
- **The edges of a side meet the node around its middle.** They sit 20 apart, as a bundle in the middle of the side, however long the side is: the layout is given 20 as the distance between ports in every graph, and the center alignment for the ports of every node. ELK keeps the ports of a group at the start of its side whatever the alignment; the mode's placement moves that bundle into the middle of the side. The default spreads the edges over the side, which puts the outer ones where the corner turns. The 20 replace the default's label-aware distance ([0019](./0019-adaptive-flowchart-mode.md)). Two labeled edges between the same two nodes, the only edges of their sides, are the exception (see below): where both then run straight, their ports spread as far apart as their labels stand, with 12 left at the ends of the side.
- **Spacing is wider.** The base value of the root's spacings is 48 where the reference has 40. A group pads its members by 32, and by 44 at the top, where its title has a row of its own, 12 below the frame's edge. A group that draws no title, a concurrency region, has 32 on every side; a note's group, which draws nothing, keeps its padding.
- **Nodes stand on axes.** ELK decides the layers and the order of the nodes in them; where the nodes stand along their layer is the mode's own placement, run after ELK's network simplex placement in every graph. An edge that is in the middle of its side at both of its nodes is an axis edge: the edge of a chain, the middle edge of a fan. Its nodes stand on one line and it runs straight, before any other edge does. Of two edges in the middle of an even side, the one that runs with the flow and passes fewer layers is the axis edge, and the bundle moves by half the distance between two edges, so that the axis edge leaves from the middle of the node; a main edge beside the middle of an odd side takes the axis from an edge against the flow. A node between two equals stands in their middle. Two edges between the same two nodes through the same layers that both carry a label and are the only edges of their sides are twins: neither is the axis, and their labels stand as close as they may on both sides of the line through the nodes' middles. Where the sides have room for it, their ports spread as far as the labels stand apart and both edges run straight; where the labels are too long for that, the ports stay a bundle in the middle and both edges turn out alike. Ports that spread without making their edges straight would only move the turns to the corners of the nodes. A twin without a label stays the axis edge. An edge between two nodes counts for more than the end of a long edge, and an edge against the flow for less, so the main flow lines up and what goes around it bends. The placement is ferrigura's own and lives in the renderer, not in the ELK port: the port hands it the layers as plain data after its own placement and takes the places back (`ferrigura_elk::placement`), so the port stays a port ([0012](./0012-layout-engines-ported-exactly.md)) under its own license ([0013](./0013-licenses-of-ported-code.md)). The default and `compat` never run it. A `nodePlacementStrategy` in the configuration is the user's choice and stays, without the mode's placement.
- **A decision is a flat diamond.** The reference's decision is a square on its tip whose side is the label's width and height together, the largest element of most charts. The mode draws a rhombus wider than tall: for a label of `w` by `h` it is `2 h + 26` high, so the label takes a little less than half of it, and as wide as the label with 16 on each side needs to fit (`w / W + h / H <= 1`), at least the width of a box of the minimum width (152). A label of one line makes it 60 high, against 44 for a box. The label keeps its own width; the outline, its box and the clipping of its edges are the rhombus.
- **A branch beside a decision leaves from its side tip.** ELK routes every edge out of the side of a node that faces the next layer, so a branch to a node beside the decision runs down, across and down again. After the layout, a step of the mode takes such a branch out of the decision's side tip, across to where the old route entered the target, and down into it: one turn instead of two. In a chart from top to bottom the tips are left and right, from left to right top and bottom; all four directions are handled. The step applies to an edge out of a decision into a node that stands beyond the decision along the flow with no node between them, whose old route enters the target along the flow on its near side and stays between the decision's middle and the target, and whose entry lies at least 20 beyond the side tip, so that the turn keeps most of its radius. Every other edge keeps its route, and so does a branch when its label would not sit on the last part of the new route (at least half a turn's room past the turn), when the new route would pass another node or another edge's label within 4, when it would leave the group of its ends or enter another group, when its ends are in different groups or in a group with a direction of its own, and when it is a self-loop, invisible, or ends at a group. One branch takes a tip: the one whose target is nearest, unless its new route meets more edges than its old one, in which case the next one tries. The edge straight on along the axis keeps leaving from the tip that faces the flow, and incoming edges are not changed. The step is the renderer's own and runs on the applied layout, not in the ELK port.
- **Corners and shadows are soft.** A rectangle node has a corner radius of 8, a group frame of 12. The look's drop shadow is short and blurred, and a group frame has none.
- **A group title keeps clear of the edges.** It sits at the left of its frame, 16 from the edge, and moves right past every edge that crosses its row. When the row has no free place wide enough, it stays at the left.
- **Contrast is checked.** In both sets, text reaches 4.5 to 1 on its ground, and outlines and edges reach 3 to 1 on the background and inside a group (WCAG 2.1, 1.4.3 and 1.4.11). The background is the mode's `background` variable: the mode cannot know the page behind a transparent SVG.
- **A `classDef` or `style` of the diagram keeps the last word.** The mode's rules come before the class rules.

Flowcharts and state diagrams support the mode. Another diagram type returns an option error when the mode is requested, as an unsupported draw animation does ([0021](./0021-optional-svg-draw-animation.md)).

A state diagram goes through the flowchart's pipeline and engine, so the measures, the placement, the edges and the labels are the flowchart's. What only a state diagram has is decided for it:

- **A state is a node.** It has the outline, the corner radius and the shadow of a flowchart's rectangle, and its label is centered, where the default starts it at the left of the box. A note keeps its text at the left.
- **A composite state is a group.** It is neutral, with the state's outline, a corner radius of 12 and no shadow, and its title stands in a row of its own at the left. The reference's title bar is not drawn.
- **A concurrency region is a dashed frame without a fill**, with a corner radius of 8, inside its composite state. The regions of a composite stand in one row, side by side across their flow, however many there are, unless the states of every region run from left to right or back: then they stand in one column. Regions stacked along the flow would read as steps one after the other. ELK places the regions, the separate parts of the composite's graph, in rows no wider than the square root of their area times the aspect ratio; the mode gives the composite a ratio no row reaches, or 0 for the column. The default keeps ELK's ratio of 1.6, which puts two regions side by side or one above the other by a few points of their size.
- **A note is flat paper:** a gray fill, a hairline outline, square corners, muted text and no shadow, so that it does not read as a state. Its dashed connector is the reference's.
- **Each transition into an end state meets an end state of its own.** The end of a scope is one node, and every transition that ends the scope would cross the drawing to meet the others at a circle too small for their arrowheads. A final state may be drawn more than once; each copy stands beside the state it ends.
- **The choice keeps its size.** It grows with its edges in no direction, as the flowchart's diamond does not. Its branch to a state beside it leaves from its side tip, as a decision's does.
- **The arrowhead** is the flowchart's, drawn under the ids of the reference's barb.

`design` and `compat` exclude each other; the combination returns an option error. The design mode builds on the optimized default and is free to depart from the reference's geometry.

## Why

The default look follows Mermaid's, which reads as generated: every stroke has the same weight, color cycles with the order of the elements, and a dark scheme is white outlines on black. A diagram that looks designed needs choices the reference does not make. A separate mode keeps both promises: the default stays comparable with the reference, and the design mode is free to differ.

A render option instead of a theme name or a `look` value keeps diagram texts portable: the same text renders in Mermaid, in ferrigura's default and in the design mode. Laying the colors under the user's variables keeps one path for colors and keeps overrides working.

The reference sets a diagram tightly: four edges meet a node 41 high, and a group's title shares its row with the edges that enter the group. A drawing that reads as designed gives lines room. Growing the node, not only spreading its ports, is what a person drawing it by hand would do.

The measures are given to the engine as its own options and as node sizes, the way the adaptive port policy is ([0019](./0019-adaptive-flowchart-mode.md)). Changing the engine would end its comparison with the reference; changing its input does not.

A decision drawn as a large square dominates a chart that it should only punctuate, and its two-bend branches read as generated: a person draws a branch out of the side, across and down. The flat diamond keeps the shape that says "decision" at the height of a row of boxes; the side exit is a rewrite of a finished route, not a layout rule, because ELK's routing has no notion of a side tip, and the conditions keep it to routes that it can rewrite without passing anything.

The title moves because the centered title is where edges enter a frame from above. The fix belongs to this mode: the default reproduces the reference's title, and changing it there needs a patch on the reference ([0017](./0017-reproduce-the-reference.md)).

Weighed and not chosen: changing the default look, which breaks the comparison with the reference for every user; and a shipped theme pair under a new `theme` name, which cannot carry stroke widths, radii, shadows or roles.

## Consequences

- The reference has no counterpart to this mode, and its geometry is not compared with the reference's. The engine that lays it out is: the default and the compatibility rendering run the same code against the reference. The mode's own tests cover the flat decision and the side exits of its branches, the room a node gives its edges, the group's padding and title, the roles, both color schemes, the contrast ratios and the precedence of the user's styles.
- A diagram in the mode is larger than in the default rendering.
- A diagram type gains the mode in its style sheet and shapes; the option error lists nothing and names no types, so adding a type changes no message.
- Roles are read from the shape and the diagram type alone. A diagram that uses one of those shapes for every node is colored throughout.
