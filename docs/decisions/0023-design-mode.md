# 0023 A design mode beside the reference's look

**Scope:** the per-render option that draws a diagram in ferrigura's own look and with its own measures, what both change, and how the mode relates to the compatibility switch. It does not change parsing, Mermaid configuration, text measurement or the layout engines.

## Decision

`RenderOptions.design` selects the design mode. It defaults to `false`, and an absent option preserves the output byte for byte. Node.js takes `design: true`; the CLI takes `--design`. It is a render option, not a Mermaid configuration key: front matter and init directives cannot set it.

The mode has its own look and its own measures. The look is paint: colors, outlines, corners, arrowheads. The measures change what the layout engine is given: node sizes, spacings, paddings and options. The engines themselves stay exact ports ([0012](./0012-layout-engines-ported-exactly.md)), and the mode starts from the optimized default's layout input ([0019](./0019-adaptive-flowchart-mode.md)).

- **Colors are theme variables.** The mode lays its own values for the shared color tokens under the user's `themeVariables`, which win key by key. Elements and custom properties keep reading the theme tokens ([0011](./0011-themes-as-css-variables.md)). It has a light and a dark set: the color scheme chooses, and without one the configured theme's name does.
- **Color follows a node's role, not its order.** A shape with a conventional meaning carries a `data-role` attribute that the style sheet colors. In a flowchart: `terminal` for the stadium and the circles, `decision` for the diamond, `data` for the cylinder. In a state diagram: `terminal` for the start and the end state, `decision` for the choice, and `junction` for the fork and the join bar, which take the color of the lines they join. In a class diagram: `interface` for an interface or an abstract class and `enumeration` for an enumeration, read from the class's annotation. The roles are the diagram type's: a shape both types can draw carries the role of the type it is drawn in. Other shapes stay neutral. The theme's palette is not used: groups are neutral.
- **Outlines are hairlines.** A node outline is 1.25 px and an edge 1.5 px, so that nodes stand in front of their connections. A thick edge is 3 px.
- **Edges are drawn anew through the points of their route.** A rounded edge turns on a radius of 10 instead of 5, also where it hops over another edge. It is drawn without the points the layout leaves on its straight parts: a corner takes at most half of each part that meets in it, so a point shortly before a corner would make that turn tighter than the others. The part at an end of an edge counts from its node, before the arrowhead pulls the end back, and the corner takes no more than the part drawn, so an arrowhead does not tighten the turn before it either. The arrowhead is slimmer than the reference's and notched at the back; its tip stays on the end of the path. An edge label interrupts its edge: the edges are masked in the label's box, which is its text's box and 5 more on each side with a corner radius of 6, so the label needs no background and reads on any ground. The layout is given that box's width and 6 of room at both ends, so that it does not touch a node beside it. A group's frame is pulled in around its members and the boxes of the labels of its inner edges after the layout; where it still crosses a label's box, the label slides along the straight part of its edge, the shorter way, until the box is 6 clear of the frame.
- **A node gives its edges room.** A node grows along the side its edges meet until its busiest side has 20 for each edge and 24 at both ends: along its height in a layout from left to right, along its width from top to bottom. The edges into a node count for one side, the edges out of it for the other. A shape that takes no size of its own, such as the stadium and the diamond, stays as it is.
- **The edges of a side meet the node around its middle.** They sit 20 apart, as a bundle in the middle of the side, however long the side is: the layout is given 20 as the distance between ports in every graph, and the center alignment for the ports of every node. ELK keeps the ports of a group at the start of its side whatever the alignment; the mode's placement moves that bundle into the middle of the side. Where ELK spreads the ports of a group unevenly over a side whose edges are a fan of equals (see below), they become a bundle 20 apart in the middle of the side: the middle edge runs straight and the outer ones turn alike. Where the side has room for every edge to run straight, with 12 left at its ends and 20 between two ports, the ports move to where their edges meet the next layer instead. The default spreads the edges over the side, which puts the outer ones where the corner turns. The 20 replace the default's label-aware distance ([0019](./0019-adaptive-flowchart-mode.md)). Two labeled edges between the same two nodes, the only edges of their sides, are the exception (see below): where both then run straight, their ports spread as far apart as their labels stand, with 12 left at the ends of the side.
- **Spacing is wider.** The base value of the root's spacings is 48 where the reference has 40. In every graph, the root and the groups, a layer stands 30 from the first turn of the edges beside it (`elk.layered.spacing.edgeNodeBetweenLayers`), where the base value gives 24: a right angle on the radius 10 starts 14.1 before its bend and takes at most half of a part, so a part next to a node needs 28.3 for the full radius, and one of 24 turned on about 8.5. Every corner of a route with room for it then turns on the same radius, and the radius stays the curve it reads as; the price is a diagram a little taller where edges turn between two layers, 12 per such gap at the root (a group had 30 already), while a gap whose edges all run straight keeps its 48. A smaller radius everywhere, 8.5, would have kept the height and made every turn read less as a curve. The 30 is what the reference gives a group, and the reach within which the glue straightens a step beside a node ([flowchart](../diagrams/flowchart.md)); a larger value would leave such steps. A group pads its members by 32, and by 44 at the top, where its title has a row of its own, 12 below the frame's edge. A group that draws no title, a concurrency region, has 32 on every side; a note's group, which draws nothing, keeps its padding.
- **Nodes stand on axes.** ELK decides the layers and the order of the nodes in them; where the nodes stand along their layer is the mode's own placement, run after ELK's network simplex placement in every graph. An edge that is in the middle of its side at both of its nodes is an axis edge: the edge of a chain, the middle edge of a fan. Its nodes stand on one line and it runs straight, before any other edge does. A chain stays one axis where it crosses the frame of a subgraph, a composite state or a namespace: the group is laid out before the graph around it and places the way on inside, so the port where the edge crosses the frame counts as the middle of the group's side and stays where the group's layout put it, and the node outside stands on its line. Of two edges in the middle of an even side, the one that runs with the flow and passes fewer layers is the axis edge, and the bundle moves by half the distance between two edges, so that the axis edge leaves from the middle of the node; a main edge beside the middle of an odd side takes the axis from an edge against the flow. A node between two equals stands in their middle. So does a node over an even fan of equals, two or four edges of a side to nodes that meet no other edge on their side towards it, even where one of those nodes has more below it than the other: a parent stands centered over its two children, and both edges turn alike, by at least 20, which a short step would show as a jog. Its group moves, or the groups of the children all move, where that bends no other edge; a fan that cannot stand symmetric is placed as any other side. A node on a chain whose two edges lead one on and one to an end is a chain with a branch beside it, not a fan: the chain runs straight on, and the branch turns. Two edges between the same two nodes through the same layers that both carry a label and are the only edges of their sides are twins: neither is the axis, and their labels stand as close as they may on both sides of the line through the nodes' middles. Where the sides have room for it, their ports spread as far as the labels stand apart and both edges run straight; where the labels are too long for that, the ports stay a bundle in the middle and both edges turn out alike. Ports that spread without making their edges straight would only move the turns to the corners of the nodes. A twin without a label stays the axis edge. Two such labeled edges beside other edges of their sides are one unit when the axes are chosen: where a side's axis would go to an edge beside the pair, it goes to the pair, so the other node stands below the pair and the other edges go beside it, instead of both edges of the pair taking a long way around another edge on the axis. They are set apart as above only where neither already runs straight, and their ports stay a bundle. An edge between two nodes counts for more than the end of a long edge, and an edge against the flow for less, so the main flow lines up and what goes around it bends. The placement is ferrigura's own and lives in the renderer, not in the ELK port: the port hands it the layers as plain data after its own placement and takes the places back (`ferrigura_elk::placement`), so the port stays a port ([0012](./0012-layout-engines-ported-exactly.md)) under its own license ([0013](./0013-licenses-of-ported-code.md)). The default and `compat` never run it. A `nodePlacementStrategy` in the configuration is the user's choice and stays, without the mode's placement.
- **Siblings stand in one layer whether or not their edges carry labels.** ELK gives the centered label of an edge a node in a layer of its own, so a labeled edge spans two layers where an edge without a label spans one: of two children of one node, the one whose edge carries a label stood a layer lower than the other, and its edge took a long way; of two parents of one node, the one whose edge carries none stood in the layer of the other's label. ELK's options do not avoid that layer: an end label (`HEAD`, `TAIL`) takes none but stands at an end of its edge, not in its gap, and the center label placement strategy only chooses which layer of a long edge holds the label. The mode therefore gives an edge without a label a label of no size and no drawing, which holds a layer as a label does, where its source has a labeled edge to another node or its target a labeled edge from another node, unless one of those two other ends reaches the other along the edges: then they are no siblings, and lengthening the edge would only push the one after the other further down. The layout input changes, ELK does not. Siblings then stand in one layer, and each label in the gap of its edge.
- **A decision is a flat diamond.** The reference's decision is a square on its tip whose side is the label's width and height together, the largest element of most charts. The mode draws a rhombus wider than tall: for a label of `w` by `h` it is `2 h + 26` high, so the label takes a little less than half of it, and as wide as the label with 16 on each side needs to fit (`w / W + h / H <= 1`), at least the width of a box of the minimum width (152). A label of one line makes it 60 high, against 44 for a box. The label keeps its own width; the outline, its box and the clipping of its edges are the rhombus.
- **A branch beside a decision leaves from its side tip.** ELK routes every edge out of the side of a node that faces the next layer, so a branch to a node beside the decision runs down, across and down again. After the layout, a step of the mode takes such a branch out of the decision's side tip, across to where the old route entered the target, and down into it: one turn instead of two. In a chart from top to bottom the tips are left and right, from left to right top and bottom; all four directions are handled. The step applies to an edge out of a decision into a node that stands beyond the decision along the flow with no node between them, whose old route enters the target along the flow on its near side and stays between the decision's middle and the target, and whose entry lies at least 20 beyond the side tip, so that the turn keeps most of its radius. Every other edge keeps its route, and so does a branch when its label would not sit on the last part of the new route (at least half a turn's room past the turn), when the new route would pass another node or another edge's label within 4, when it would leave the group of its ends or enter another group, when its ends are in different groups or in a group with a direction of its own, when a self-loop of the decision stands at that tip, and when it is a self-loop, invisible, or ends at a group. One branch takes a tip: the one whose target is nearest, unless its new route meets more edges than its old one, in which case the next one tries. The edge straight on along the axis keeps leaving from the tip that faces the flow, and incoming edges are not changed. The step is the renderer's own and runs on the applied layout, not in the ELK port.
- **A self-loop stands on a free side of its node.** ELK draws a loop as a small hook near a corner of its node and puts its label beyond the hook, away from the line. In the mode, a loop leaves the side of its node that no other edge meets and comes back into it: the right side in a layout from top to bottom or back, the top in a layout from left to right or back, and a second loop the opposite side. A decision's loop stays off a side tip that one of its branches would leave from: it takes the other tip, and where its branches need both, the side that faces the next layer, below the decision in a layout from top to bottom; the diagram is laid out again for that, and where the loop does not fit there, it keeps its first side. Its ends meet the side 20 apart around its middle (half the side apart on a shape too small for that, such as a state's choice), it stands 32 out and turns on the edge's radius, and its label's box stands 4 beside it, centered on it: past the loop on the right and the left, at its right on the top and the bottom. A class's cardinalities stand beside their ends of the loop, and its label past the cardinality at the end. A node keeps its size for its loop: on a side shorter than 40 the ends are half the side apart, so that a state with a loop stands as high as its neighbors. The loops take no part in ELK's layout: their node keeps their room on the loop's side only, as a label of the node at a fixed place beside it, which ELK holds in the node's margins without moving the node's ports off its own sides; the room reaches along the side as far as the label or the cardinalities need. After ELK's layout, before the result is applied, each loop goes into the room beside its node; the result is applied as ELK's routes are, so the ends are clipped at the outline of the node's shape. More loops than free sides share them, the first side the first two: two loops on one side stand one after the other along it, each a quarter of the side from its middle with its ends a quarter of the side apart (at most 20), their labels past them and at least 4 apart, or at their outer ends on the top and the bottom. Where a state's notes take both free sides, its loop stands on the side its transitions enter, its end 4 from the far corner, its label past it. A group's loop, on a subgraph or a composite state, stands on its frame as a node's does; since its room is a box at a fixed place from the frame's corner, layouts before give the frame's size: a first one without the loops of groups, and one more for each level of groups with loops inside other groups with loops. A node with more than two loops on a free side, with an invisible loop, or with its notes on both sides and more than one loop keeps ELK's loops. Where a loop, its label or a cardinality would touch a node, an edge, a label or a frame, or stand outside the node's groups, the diagram is laid out again with that node's loops on the sides of the other direction, since a node in a group with a direction of its own can meet its edges in the direction of the group's parent, and then with ELK's loops. The step is the renderer's own and runs around ELK's layout, not in the ELK port.
- **Corners and shadows are soft.** A rectangle node has a corner radius of 8, a group frame of 12. The look's drop shadow is short and blurred, and a group frame has none.
- **A group title keeps clear of the edges.** It sits at the left of its frame, 16 from the edge, and moves right past every edge that crosses its row. When the row has no free place wide enough, it stays at the left.
- **Contrast is checked.** In both sets, text reaches 4.5 to 1 on its ground, and outlines and edges reach 3 to 1 on the background and inside a group, and a box's divider on the box's ground (WCAG 2.1, 1.4.3 and 1.4.11). So do the series palette, its tints and the charts' own colors. The background is the mode's `background` variable: the mode cannot know the page behind a transparent SVG.
- **A word stays whole in a label.** A label wraps between words as the default does, but a word that does not fit the wrapping width on a line of its own is not broken inside: the line stays one line wider than the wrapping width, and its node grows as wide as the word needs, with the node's usual padding. This holds for the labels of nodes and states, edge labels and Markdown group titles (a plain group title never wraps); a class box does not wrap its rows. A word is what stands between spaces: no break point is added at a hyphen, a slash or a camel-case boundary. A word wider than 4 times the wrapping width, such as a pasted URL, is still broken as the default breaks it, so that one label cannot blow a diagram up (`WHOLE_WORD_LIMIT` in `design.rs`). The default and `compat` break the word, as the reference does.
- **A `classDef` or `style` of the diagram keeps the last word.** The mode's rules come before the class rules.

Every diagram type supports the mode. Flowcharts, state diagrams, class diagrams and ER diagrams take its look and its measures; sequence diagrams and mindmaps take its look on a layout of their own; the pie, XY, quadrant and gantt charts take its look alone (see below).

A state diagram goes through the flowchart's pipeline and engine, so the measures, the placement, the edges and the labels are the flowchart's. What only a state diagram has is decided for it:

- **A state is a node.** It has the outline, the corner radius and the shadow of a flowchart's rectangle, and its label is centered, where the default starts it at the left of the box. A note keeps its text at the left. A state keeps its least width of 136; a text that fills it has 16 to the outline at each side, the room of a flowchart's node, where the default leaves 8. A state with descriptions has the state's outline and corners, 16 at its sides, 10 above its title, between its title and divider, between the divider and its rows, and below its last row, its rows at the left, and is at least as wide as a plain state.
- **A composite state is a group.** It is neutral, with the state's outline, a corner radius of 12 and no shadow, and its title stands in a row of its own at the left. The reference's title bar is not drawn.
- **A concurrency region is a dashed frame without a fill**, with a corner radius of 8, inside its composite state. The regions of a composite stand in one row, side by side across their flow, however many there are, unless the states of every region run from left to right or back: then they stand in one column. Regions stacked along the flow would read as steps one after the other. ELK places the regions, the separate parts of the composite's graph, in rows no wider than the square root of their area times the aspect ratio; the mode gives the composite a ratio no row reaches, or 0 for the column. The default keeps ELK's ratio of 1.6, which puts two regions side by side or one above the other by a few points of their size.
- **A note is flat paper:** a gray fill, a hairline outline, square corners, muted text and no shadow, so that it does not read as a state. Its dashed connector is the reference's.
- **A note stands beside its state, at the state's height.** The reference lays a note out as a node of its own, which ELK puts in a layer of its own below or above its state, so the states of the main flow move off their axis around it. The mode leaves the notes out of ELK's layout and keeps their room beside their state instead, on the note's side only: the note's box, 32 from the state's side, goes to ELK as a label of the state at that fixed place, which ELK holds in the state's margins, while the state keeps its own size and its transitions meet its own sides. After ELK's layout, before the result is applied, the note goes into that room, 32 from its side and centered on it, with a straight dashed connector. In a layout from top to bottom, `left of` is to the left and `right of` to the right; from left to right, where the transitions meet the left and the right side, the note keeps its place along the layer as ELK turns the axes: `left of` above the state, `right of` below. The note's group belongs to the state's parent, so a composite state's frame holds the notes of its states. A note on a composite state stands beside its frame the same way, 32 from it and centered on it; since its room is a box at a fixed place from the frame's corner, a first layout with the notes of composites out of it gives the frame's size. Two or more notes on one side of a state stand in a column along that side, 8 apart and centered together on the state, each with a straight connector across the stretch over which it stands beside the state. A state with a transition to itself takes the loop on the side without a note: a note `right of` it in a layout from top to bottom puts its loop on the left. A state keeps its notes where the reference puts them when it has an invisible transition to itself, when a note of a column would stand beside it over less than 8, or when one of its notes would touch a node, a transition, a label or a frame, or stand outside its composite: the diagram is laid out again with that state's notes in the reference's place. The side without a note keeps no room, so a state with a note stands as close to its neighbors on that side as one without. The step is the renderer's own and runs around ELK's layout, not in the ELK port.
- **Each transition into an end state meets an end state of its own.** The end of a scope is one node, and every transition that ends the scope would cross the drawing to meet the others at a circle too small for their arrowheads. A final state may be drawn more than once; each copy stands beside the state it ends.
- **The choice keeps its size.** It grows with its edges in no direction, as the flowchart's diamond does not. Its branch to a state beside it leaves from its side tip, as a decision's does.
- **The arrowhead** is the flowchart's, drawn under the ids of the reference's barb.

A class diagram goes through the same pipeline and engine, so the measures, the placement, the edges and the labels are the flowchart's. What only a class diagram has is decided for it ([class diagram](../diagrams/class.md#design-mode)):

- **A class box draws only the compartments it has.** The name stands bold and centered in its own compartment, the attributes and the methods follow in theirs, divided by 1 px hairlines across the box. A class without members is its name alone, where the reference draws two empty compartments as tall as a row. The box has the outline, the corner radius 8 and the shadow of a flowchart's rectangle, 16 of room at the sides of its rows and a width of at least 120.
- **The annotation is a quiet line above the name**, at 0.875 of the font size in the muted title color. The members start at the left; their visibility marks stand muted in a column of their own, so that the names start on one line. A static member is underlined, an abstract one set in italics. A row is plain text: a generic type shows its angle brackets, and Markdown is not read.
- **Two roles, read from the annotation.** An interface or an abstract class, a type that is not instantiated, takes a cool tint and outline (`interface`); an enumeration, a set of values, a green one (`enumeration`). Every other class is neutral, and the palette is not used: a color per class says nothing, and a service or an entity looks like any class. Fewer roles keep the tint a signal.
- **The markers are drawn in the gap the reference leaves for them**, the edge stopping at the marker's offset before the outline: a hollow triangle for an inheritance and a realization, a filled and a hollow diamond for a composition and an aggregation, an open arrow for an association and a dependency, a ring for a lollipop. They take the outline's hairline and round joins, their tips meet the outline, and a hollow one is filled with the box's ground, so they read in both schemes. The edges of a side meet it around its middle, so no marker meets a rounded corner.
- **The general end stands above.** A relation with a triangle or a diamond at its second end and none at its first is laid out from the other end, its ends, markers and cardinalities changing places: `Dog --|> Animal` puts the parent above as `Animal <|-- Dog` does, and `Wheel --* Car` the whole above its part. ELK lays an edge out from its start to its end, so the written direction would otherwise decide.
- **The children of a class share one triangle.** After the layout, the generalizations that meet one side of a class with the same line pattern are merged: each route runs on to the line of the turn nearest the class, along it to the middle of the side, or to the line of a child that stands straight below, and into the class. Where another edge meets the class near the middle of the side, such as a composition whose part stands in the children's layer, the triangle stands in the middle of its edges' ends instead. A side where that place is taken too, a route that is not orthogonal, a relation with a label and an end with a cardinality keep their own routes. The step is the renderer's own and runs on the applied layout, not in the ELK port.
- **A cardinality stands beside its end**, 4 off the outline, beside the marker and 4 clear of it, on the side away from the middle of the class's side, so that the cardinalities of two edges into one side stand outside the pair. Where that place is taken it takes the other side of its line, then steps outward along its segment. The reference's passes for the cardinalities do not run in the mode.
- **A namespace is the flowchart's group**, and a note is the state diagram's flat paper.

An ER diagram goes through the same pipeline and engine, so the measures, the placement, the edges and the labels are the flowchart's. What only an ER diagram has is decided for it ([ER diagram](../diagrams/er.md#design-mode)):

- **An entity box is a name and its rows.** The name stands bold and centered in a row of its own; the attributes follow below a 1 px hairline, each row divided from the next by a fainter one, without the reference's alternating fills. An entity without attributes is its name alone, centered in its box however much the box grows for its edges. The box has the outline, the corner radius 8 and the shadow of a flowchart's rectangle, 16 of room at the sides of its rows and a width of at least 120, the class box's.
- **An attribute's name leads its row.** The columns are the name, the type, the keys and the comment, in that order, each starting on one line in every row, 16 apart; a column that no attribute fills takes no room. The name is in the text color; the type, the keys (smaller and bold) and the comment (smaller) are muted. A row is plain text: a generic type shows its angle brackets, and Markdown is not read.
- **No entity carries a role.** Every entity is one kind of thing, and the syntax names no weak or associative entity, so every entity is neutral and the palette is not used. What a relation says, its line and its ends say.
- **A relation's line says whether it identifies.** An identifying relation is solid, a non-identifying one dashed (5 on, 4 off), at the edge's weight; the reference's dash array, which its neo look writes on the path, gives way to the mode's.
- **The cardinality markers are drawn back along the line from the outline**, which an ER relation meets without an offset: a bar for one, a ring for zero, a crow's foot for many, whose three prongs meet the outline, the middle one on the line, so that the foot is whole on a dashed line. They take the edge's stroke and color with flat ends, and the ring is filled with the page's ground, so they read in both schemes. ELK keeps the turns of a relation 36 from its entities, the longest marker with the corner's radius, and a relation to itself stands 36 out, so that every marker stands on a straight end of its line.
- **A relation's label sits in a gap of its line**, muted, as a class relation's does, and a subgraph is the flowchart's group.

The pie, XY, quadrant and gantt charts take the mode's look and keep their layout, since they have nothing to lay out; a mindmap takes the same look on the mode's own layout (see below). Their look is paint, in theme variables of the reference's own names, laid under the user's as the other colors are, so a user's `pie2`, `cScale1`, `quadrant1Fill` or `taskBkgColor` wins; a user's `xyChart` group is merged into the mode's key by key. What these types share is decided once:

- **A series takes one calm palette.** Where a type has a series, its parts take the colors of the mode's series palette in its fixed order, never the theme's cycling scale: blue, teal, amber, violet, green, rose, cyan and brick (`SERIES_LIGHT` and `SERIES_DARK` in `design.rs`), wrapping after the eighth. Every color is muted and of one lightness: in the light set dark enough for white text, in the dark set light enough for the dark background's color as text (`series_text`); both reach 3 to 1 on the background. Neighbors, the eighth and the first included, stay apart for a reader with a red-green deficiency, the order chosen among all orders for that, and a chart gives a series its legend or its gaps too. A box of a series takes the color's tint, the color at 12 percent on the light background or 18 on the dark (`SERIES_TINT_LIGHT`, `SERIES_TINT_DARK`), with the text color and the color as its outline. Another type with a series reuses the palette.
- **A pie's slices are the series colors** at full opacity, parted by 2 px of the background instead of black outlines, without the outer ring; the percentages are the series' text color, the title and the legend the text color at 18 and 14 px.
- **An XY chart's plots are the series colors** in the order of the plots; a text that gives its own `plotColorPalette` keeps it. Axis titles and labels are the muted title color, axis lines and ticks the outline's gray. A data label is the series' text color on its bar and the text color beside it, unless the theme gives `dataLabelColor`.
- **A quadrant chart's quadrants are the tints of the first four colors**, parted by the background instead of outlines, with muted labels; its points take the first color.
- **A gantt section's bars take its series color**: the tint as the fill and the color as a hairline outline, for the four section classes the reference names. The parts with a meaning take a role instead: a done task is neutral gray, a critical one a red the palette does not use, also for the today marker; an active task keeps its section's colors with a heavier outline. The section bands lose the reference's opacity and alternate between a quiet gray and the background, and the tick labels are muted. A user's task colors color every bar alike, as in the default.
- **A mindmap's branches take the series colors**, from the second on: a node takes its branch's tint with a hairline in its color, its edges the color, thinner with each level from 2.5 to 1.5. The root takes the flowchart's terminal blue, the first color of the palette.
- **The XY and the quadrant chart write their colors as presentation attributes**, so `colorScheme: auto` draws them in the light set, as the default does; the pie, the gantt chart and the mindmap write both sets as `light-dark()` values.

A mindmap is laid out by a layout of the mode's own, not by cose-bilkent, the reference's force-directed engine, whose branches land where its forces settle ([mindmap](../diagrams/mindmap.md#design-mode)):

- **A tree around its center, clockwise.** The root's first branches stand on its right from top to bottom, the rest on its left from bottom to top, so that the order of the text runs around the root; the split is the one that makes the taller side the least tall, and of equal ones the one with more on the right.
- **Each side grows outwards in columns.** A node's children stand beyond it in the order of the text, their inner sides on one line, 40 from the node; the root's branches 64 from the root, or further where a side has many, so that no line from the root rises more than 3 for 1 across. A node stands in the middle between its first and its last child.
- **Siblings have even room.** Each subtree takes the room of its box; siblings stand 10 apart, the root's branches 24.
- **A line reads with its branch.** It leaves the middle of the parent's outer side and enters the middle of the child's inner side, level at both ends, as a `basis` curve; a line from the root starts in its center and leaves the root's box where the straight line to its branch does, so that the branches leave the root apart.
- **Depth shows in size.** The root has 5 more padding than its shape gives it, a node below a branch 6 less (at least 6). A label is centered in every shape, the cloud and the bang included, and a node does not grow for its edges.

A `layout` that names the tidy tree or ELK is the user's choice and stays. The tidy tree is not the base: it alternates the root's children between the sides and packs subtrees by their contours. The layout is deterministic ([0018](./0018-deterministic-output.md)); the default and `compat` keep cose-bilkent.

A sequence diagram has no layout engine: the renderer's own pass places its parts, as the reference's does. The mode gives it the look of the others and its own measures ([sequence diagram](../diagrams/sequence.md#design-mode)):

- **Colors are the sequence's own theme variables**, laid under the user's as the others are: `actorBkg`, `actorBorder`, `actorTextColor`, `actorLineColor`, `signalColor`, `signalTextColor`, `labelBoxBkgColor`, `labelBoxBorderColor`, `labelTextColor`, `loopTextColor`, `activationBkgColor`, `activationBorderColor` and `sequenceNumberColor`, in the light and the dark set and held to the same contrast ratios. The palette themes' colors per participant are not used: a color per column says nothing.
- **Color follows a part's role.** An `actor` is a person: its figure takes the accent of the flowchart's `terminal` in its line. A `database` is a store: its cylinder takes the `data` tint and outline. A frame that chooses, `alt` and `opt`, takes the `decision` outline and tag. An activation is a quiet cool bar. Other participants and frames are neutral.
- **Outlines are hairlines.** A participant box, a frame, an activation and a note have a 1.25 px outline, a message 1.5 px, a lifeline 1 px. A participant box has a corner radius of 8 and the soft shadow, a frame 8, a box around participants and a `rect` background 12, an activation 2.
- **A note is flat paper**, as the state diagram's: square corners, a hairline, no shadow.
- **A frame is one rounded outline.** Its sections are divided by a dashed hairline. Its keyword stands in a tag that fills the frame's corner, without the reference's cut corner and shadow.
- **A label interrupts the lifelines behind it.** The lifelines are masked in the box of each message's text and frame title, 4 around the text, as an edge is masked under its label, so the label needs no background on a box's tint or a `rect`'s color.
- **A participant's name is semibold**, measured and drawn at 600, unless the configuration gives another `actorFontWeight`.
- **The arrowhead** is the flowchart's, slim and notched, with its tip where the reference's is. A box around participants keeps the user's color as a tint at 16 % opacity, so that the messages read on it.
- **A participant's box fits its name**, with 16 at its sides and 12 above and below, at least 96 wide, where the reference's columns are at least 150 wide. The row of participants is as high as its tallest participant needs, so a diagram of boxes has no glyph's band above them. The mode draws the neo geometry whatever `look` says.
- **The columns share one pitch**, the widest box and 40, at least 150, and two neighbors stand farther apart only where a label between them needs it: its width, the wrap padding and 40, so 30 are left to each lifeline. The reference sets 50 for both (`actorMargin`); a configured value other than that stays.
- **The rows keep the reference's rhythm**, which is already even (50 for a message of one line, 35 for a frame's title row, 30 for a section's), and activations keep its measures (10 wide, a nested one 5 to the right).
- **A frame's title stands beside its tag**, 10 after it, its middle on the tag's middle, without brackets; a section's title at the same column below its divider. The reference centers them over the frame, where the lifelines cross them.

`design` and `compat` exclude each other; the combination returns an option error. The design mode builds on the optimized default and is free to depart from the reference's geometry.

## Why

The default look follows Mermaid's, which reads as generated: every stroke has the same weight, color cycles with the order of the elements, and a dark scheme is white outlines on black. A diagram that looks designed needs choices the reference does not make. A separate mode keeps both promises: the default stays comparable with the reference, and the design mode is free to differ.

A render option instead of a theme name or a `look` value keeps diagram texts portable: the same text renders in Mermaid, in ferrigura's default and in the design mode. Laying the colors under the user's variables keeps one path for colors and keeps overrides working.

The reference sets a diagram tightly: four edges meet a node 41 high, and a group's title shares its row with the edges that enter the group. A drawing that reads as designed gives lines room. Growing the node, not only spreading its ports, is what a person drawing it by hand would do.

The measures are given to the engine as its own options and as node sizes, the way the adaptive port policy is ([0019](./0019-adaptive-flowchart-mode.md)). Changing the engine would end its comparison with the reference; changing its input does not.

A decision drawn as a large square dominates a chart that it should only punctuate, and its two-bend branches read as generated: a person draws a branch out of the side, across and down. The flat diamond keeps the shape that says "decision" at the height of a row of boxes; the side exit is a rewrite of a finished route, not a layout rule, because ELK's routing has no notion of a side tip, and the conditions keep it to routes that it can rewrite without passing anything.

The title moves because the centered title is where edges enter a frame from above. The fix belongs to this mode: the default reproduces the reference's title, and changing it there needs a patch on the reference ([0017](./0017-reproduce-the-reference.md)).

A series needs colors that tell its parts apart, but the reference's scales are rainbow pastels of uneven lightness that change from type to type, so a pie and a gantt chart beside it share nothing. One palette of muted colors at one lightness reads as one hand across the types, and a fixed order means a series keeps its color between charts. The charts keep the reference's names for their colors, so a user's theme variables reach them in the mode as they do in the default.

Weighed and not chosen: changing the default look, which breaks the comparison with the reference for every user; and a shipped theme pair under a new `theme` name, which cannot carry stroke widths, radii, shadows or roles.

## Consequences

- The reference has no counterpart to this mode, and its geometry is not compared with the reference's. The engine that lays it out is: the default and the compatibility rendering run the same code against the reference. The mode's own tests cover the flat decision and the side exits of its branches, the place of a note beside its state, the place of a self-loop and its label, the room a node gives its edges, the group's padding and title, the class box's compartments, the direction of a class relation, the shared triangle, the place of a cardinality, a mindmap's order, room, lines and sizes, a sequence's participant boxes, pitch, frame titles and gaps in the lifelines, the entity box's columns, the straight ends of an ER relation, the roles, the series palette of the charts and the mindmap, both color schemes, the contrast ratios and the precedence of the user's styles.
- A graph in the mode is larger than in the default rendering; a sequence diagram with short names is narrower, as its columns fit their boxes.
- A diagram type has the mode in its style sheet and shapes, and a new type gains it there.
- Roles are read from the shape and the diagram type alone, a class's from its first annotation, a sequence participant's from its type and a frame's from its keyword. A diagram that uses one of those shapes for every node is colored throughout.
- A diagram in the mode takes longer to lay out than in the default rendering. ELK places the nodes with network simplex where the default uses Brandes & Koepf, the mode's placement solves the same graph again in each of its passes, and a node whose self-loops or notes do not stand clear lays the whole diagram out again. Most diagrams take about as long as in the default; a large one with such a node takes several times as long ([LEARNINGS](../LEARNINGS.md#elks-network-simplex-is-quadratic-and-the-port-takes-the-same-steps-faster)).
- A diagram type gains the mode in its style sheet and shapes; the option error lists nothing and names no types, so adding a type changes no message.
- Roles are read from the shape and the diagram type alone, and a class's from its first annotation. A diagram that uses one of those shapes for every node is colored throughout.
