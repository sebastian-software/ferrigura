ELK Layered is the layout engine in Ferrigura’s standard builds. The pinned Go implementation of TALA shows other ways to arrange groups and route their links. Three examples use the experimental Rust port, including a route across a group boundary and two labeled parallel routes.

Ferrigura measures and paints each pair with its default look, bundled fonts, and SVG text labels. The sheets labeled Go reference take their TALA coordinates from the [pinned Go implementation of D2 0.10.0](https://github.com/d2lang/d2/tree/b463ea5d47272f4da6e1e08012dbb15a6a4dbcde/d2layouts/d2talalayout) with seed 1. Each sheet labeled TALA in Rust is byte-identical to its Go replay. The [layout engine guide](/guide/layout-engines/) describes the selection available in a build.

## Nested services

TALA follows the groups with fewer right-angle turns and a narrower drawing. ELK keeps more of the diagram at the same height and uses less total route length. Pick according to where the drawing will be read.

## Directional workflow

The publishing workflow mostly moves in one direction. ELK uses fewer bends and shorter routes, and the drawing needs less width.

## Cyclic services

TALA shortens the routes and uses fewer turns in this service network. It also adds a crossing. In a diagram where a reader needs to trace one connection through several similar ones, that crossing matters.

<LayoutExample id="observatory-services" />

## Parallel edges

TALA grows two endpoints so their parallel routes have room. ELK holds the original node sizes. A resized box can help separate routes and can also change the balance of the surrounding diagram.

This single rectangular node and labeled loop exercise the experimental Rust implementation. Ferrigura generates both SVGs directly on this input.

## One group boundary

The route connects a worker inside a container to an external queue. Both routes are straight. ELK keeps the boxes closer together. TALA leaves more space around the worker inside its group and puts the external queue farther away.

## Two labeled parallel routes

TALA grows both boxes to space the two captions and shortens the total route length. ELK keeps the original box heights and makes a shallower drawing.

This fixture crosses four group boundaries and includes a cycle inside each group. It tests the tradeoffs on a larger input than the other examples. The two layouts arrange the groups differently, so inspect the SVGs as well as the totals.

## Reading the measurements

Route lengths are in SVG user units and measure the polylines before styling. The counts omit duplicate waypoints and shared or collinear segments. Edge-label overlaps count intersecting edge-label boxes and edge-label boxes that intersect a leaf node. The view box represents the painted SVG, including its padding. The area multiplies the rounded view-box dimensions. The drawings scale independently to fit their sheets. Open the SVG files to inspect them at their original size.

These figures describe the geometry of this corpus. Each example identifies the implementation that produced its TALA geometry. See the engine guide for the shapes that the Rust implementation can render.

## Release timing on this corpus

One run on November 28, 2026 used an Apple M1 Ultra with 64 GiB of RAM and Darwin 27.2.0 arm64. Ferrigura 0.10.0 was built in Cargo’s release profile with Rust 1.99.0 and the experimental TALA feature. The pinned D2 0.10.0 reference used Go 1.27.4, seed 1 and one worker. Each result is the median of 50 in-process calls after 10 warmups. Times are in milliseconds.
